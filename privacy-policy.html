Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 24, 2026

Otic ("the app") is an e-book reader and audiobook player that keeps your library on your device. This page explains, in plain language, what happens to your data when you use it.

The short version

Otic has no server of its own and collects nothing about you. By default, everything Otic does happens on your phone or tablet. Otic only goes online when you use a feature that needs it — syncing through your own Google Drive, searching for a book, browsing a free catalog, connecting to a server you run, downloading a link you paste, or turning an Audio Snip into text. Each one is described below. None of them sends anything to us.

What Otic stores, and where

When you import a book or audiobook, Otic reads the file(s) you pick using Android's Storage Access Framework — the standard system file/folder picker. Otic:

  • Never uploads your book or audio files anywhere. Files you import stay where you put them (on your device, an SD card, or a location you've linked such as a cloud-storage app you already have installed); books you download from a catalog or server are saved inside Otic's private storage on your device.
  • Keeps a local, on-device database of your library: book titles, authors, cover images, reading/listening progress, bookmarks, highlights, notes, and Audio Snips you create.
  • Does not require an Otic account, sign-in, or profile. If you choose to connect Otic to a server of your own (see "Your own servers and links" below), the login you enter is stored only on your device and is used only for that server.

None of this is ever sent to Otic — there is no Otic server.

Otic Sync (optional)

Otic Sync is an optional Otic Plus feature. It is off by default; nothing syncs unless you turn it on and sign in with your Google account. When you do:

  • Otic requests a single, narrow Google permission — access to its own app-data folder in your Google Drive (the drive.appdata scope). This is a private folder that only Otic can read or write; Google's own Drive interface doesn't even show it to you. Otic asks for no other Google data.
  • Otic writes one file there containing your library metadata and progress — book titles/authors (not the book files themselves), reading and listening positions, shelves, reading sessions, badges, and your highlights and notes — so your other devices can merge it in.
  • This data goes to your own Google Drive, under your Google account and Google's privacy policy. It does not go to Otic — there is no Otic server, and we never receive or see it.
  • For Otic Sync we store only the name of the Google account you chose (on your device, to show you it's connected) — no Google password or token. You can disconnect at any time in Settings → Otic Sync, which stops syncing.

Book search and free catalogs (optional)

Otic sends a request over HTTPS to a public book service only when you actively use one of these features — never in the background:

  • Adding a physical book: the title or author you type is sent to Open Library and Google Books to look up book details and covers.
  • Browsing the Catalogs tab: your searches and the pages you open are sent to Gutendex / Project Gutenberg (and its PGLAF mirrors), LibriVox / Internet Archive, or Standard Ebooks, and the books or audiobooks you choose are downloaded from them.

These requests carry what you searched for or picked and a generic app identifier (for example Otic-Android) — no account, device id, or other personal identifier. Those services handle the request under their own privacy policies.

Your own servers and links (optional)

  • OPDS catalogs and Audiobookshelf: if you add a catalog address or connect an Audiobookshelf server, Otic talks only to the address you entered. Any username, password, or access token you provide is stored only on your device, is excluded from Android backups, and is only ever sent to that server. Otic sends a password or token over plain http:// only to a server on your own network; anything on the internet needs https://. For a linked Audiobookshelf book, Otic also sends your listening position back to your server so the two stay in step.
  • Import from a link: when you paste a web address, Otic downloads that page or file (and, for an episode page, the audio file it points to).
  • Wireless Drop: while the Wireless Drop screen is open, Otic runs a small upload page on your Wi-Fi network so you can send books from a computer. It's reachable only on your local network, only through the one-time address shown on screen, and it stops when you leave that screen. Files you send go straight into your library on the device.

Audio Snips and transcription (optional)

An Audio Snip saves a short clip of the audiobook you're listening to, and can turn its words into text. The clip comes from the book's audio — Otic never records from your microphone. Android's speech recognition service requires the microphone permission even when it's given audio from a file, which is the only reason Otic asks for it.

Otic uses the on-device recognizer when your phone has one. If it doesn't, the clip is handed to your phone's speech recognition service (usually Google's), which may send that short clip to its servers to transcribe it, under that provider's own privacy policy. Otic itself never sends the clip anywhere. Snips you save stay on your device.

Third parties

Otic does not include analytics, advertising, tracking, or crash-reporting SDKs of any kind. We do not share or sell your data. The only outside services Otic contacts are the ones described above, and only when you use the feature that needs them: Google Drive (Otic Sync), Open Library and Google Books (book search), the free catalogs (Gutendex/Project Gutenberg, LibriVox/Internet Archive, Standard Ebooks), servers and links you enter yourself, and your phone's speech recognition service (Audio Snip transcription). Purchases of Otic Plus are handled by Google Play.

Permissions Otic requests, and why

  • Notifications — to show the playback control notification while an audiobook is playing, and progress for long tasks such as finding chapters.
  • Media playback / wake lock / foreground service — to keep audio playing reliably in the background and when your screen is off, and to let a long chapter scan finish.
  • Boot-completed — used only to reschedule widget refreshes and watched-folder checks; this does not involve the network.
  • Internet — used only by the features described above (Otic Sync, book search, catalogs, your own servers and links, and Wireless Drop on your local network).
  • Microphone — required by Android's speech recognition service to transcribe Audio Snips. Otic never records from the microphone. You can deny it; snips are still saved, just without text.
  • Vibration — for gentle haptic feedback, such as the sleep timer's shake-to-reset.

Otic does not request contacts, location, camera, or SMS access. (Capturing a quote from a paper book uses the system camera or document scanner, which hands Otic only the photo you take; the text is read on your device.)

Your data, your control

Deleting a book from within Otic removes it and its associated data from the app's local database. Removing a saved catalog or Audiobookshelf server deletes its stored login. Uninstalling Otic removes all app data from your device. If you used Otic Sync, a copy of your sync file remains in your own Google Drive app-data folder — because it lives in your Drive account, you control it directly and can remove it through Google (for example by disconnecting the app from your Google account). Otic holds no copy to delete on its side.

Children's privacy

Otic does not knowingly collect any information from anyone, including children, because it does not collect information at all.
